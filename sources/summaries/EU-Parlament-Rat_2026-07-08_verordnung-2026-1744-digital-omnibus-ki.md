# Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 amending Regulations (EU) 2024/1689, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2023/1230 (Digital Omnibus on AI)

Publisher: European Parliament and Council of the European Union (Official Journal L series, 24.7.2026)
Date: Adopted 8 July 2026, published 24.7.2026; enters into force on the third day after publication (PDF p. 41), which makes 27 July 2026 (derived from the publication date; the delegation dates in PDF p. 34 also refer to 27 July 2026)
Original: [EU-Parlament-Rat_2026-07-08_verordnung-2026-1744-digital-omnibus-ki.pdf](../EU-Parlament-Rat_2026-07-08_verordnung-2026-1744-digital-omnibus-ki.pdf)
Bindingness: Pflicht (law, directly applicable in all Member States)

## Core statement
The regulation is an amending act. It does not restate the AI Act but changes it in targeted ways: it softens the AI literacy duty, postpones the high-risk obligations, adds two prohibitions, extends SME relief and centralises enforcement for general-purpose-model-based systems at the AI Office. For DHCraft as a small company the practically relevant changes concern AI literacy (Art. 4), the new Art. 5 prohibitions, the dates of application, the Art. 50(2) transitional period and the SME and small mid-cap relief. The text contains no rule specific to AI agents, apart from one code list entry.

## Relevance to the Leitfrage
### Client projects
- Art. 4 AI Act is replaced. Providers and deployers "shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of their staff and of other persons operating or using AI systems on their behalf, considering knowledge, experience, context and the persons on whom the systems are used. The duty to ensure a sufficient level is dropped: it "does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual" (PDF p. 16, Art. 1 point 5). Recital 8 gives the reason as compliance burden for smaller enterprises (PDF p. 3).
- Candidate rule: DHCraft documents measures that build AI literacy for staff and for external persons working with AI systems on its behalf, such as freelancers or client staff in joint projects.
- Art. 2(7) AI Act is restated: Union data protection law applies to personal data processed in connection with the regulation, and the regulation does not affect the GDPR (PDF p. 15). The regulation therefore gives no legal basis for processing client personal data with AI.
- Art. 27(4) and (5): where a deployer must do a fundamental rights impact assessment, it may cross-reference an existing GDPR data protection impact assessment, and the AI Office is to provide a questionnaire template (PDF p. 20). This only matters where DHCraft deploys a high-risk system.
- New Art. 4a allows exceptional processing of special categories of personal data for bias detection and correction, under conditions such as necessity, pseudonymisation, access controls, no transfer to third parties and deletion (PDF p. 17). It extends to providers and deployers of other AI systems and creates no duty to do bias detection (PDF p. 17).

### Research
The source says nothing specific on this field. Art. 4a on special categories of personal data for bias detection (PDF p. 17) is the only research-adjacent provision, and only where bias detection is done.

### Programming
- New prohibitions in Art. 5(1)(ba) and (bb): AI systems that generate or manipulate realistic intimate images, video or audio of an identifiable person without explicit consent, and systems that generate or manipulate child sexual abuse material (PDF p. 18). For providers the ban applies where this is the intended purpose, or where it is a reasonably foreseeable and reproducible outcome and no reasonable and adequate safeguards exist. For deployers it applies where they use the system for that purpose (PDF p. 18, Art. 5(1a)). Recital 12 lists safeguards such as data cleaning, refusal training, runtime guardrails and output filters (PDF p. 5).
- Candidate rule: DHCraft does not build or deploy generative tools that can produce such material, and applies output controls to any image or video generation component it builds.
- Art. 50(2) marking of synthetic audio, image, video or text content: providers of systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must comply by 2 December 2026 (PDF p. 35, Art. 111(4); recital 38 on PDF p. 12). The code of practice mechanism in Art. 50(7) is retained, but the Commission may adopt an implementing act with common rules if the code is judged inadequate (PDF p. 22).
- Annex XIV lists AI technology codes for notified bodies, including "AI systems based on other emerging AI technologies not covered by other codes, including Agentic AI" (PDF p. 38). This is a classification code for conformity assessment bodies, not an obligation on agent users.

### Organisation
- Dates of application: the prohibitions of Art. 5 apply since 2 February 2025, with the new points (ba) and (bb) and Art. 5(1a) and (1b) applying from 2 December 2026 (PDF p. 35, Art. 113(a)). High-risk obligations in Chapter III Sections 1 to 3 apply from 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and from 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems (PDF p. 35; recital 40 on PDF p. 12).
- Article 111(2) is rephrased: operators of high-risk systems placed on the market before the application date are covered only if the design is significantly changed, and providers and deployers of high-risk systems for public authorities must comply by 2 August 2030 (PDF p. 35).
- SMEs and small mid-cap enterprises receive definitions and relief: simplified technical documentation form (PDF p. 19), proportionate quality management (PDF pp. 19, 26), simplified quality management for all SMEs (PDF p. 26), priority sandbox access (PDF pp. 23 to 24), and a rule that fines for SMCs are the lower of the percentage or the amount (PDF p. 35). Member States must consider SME interests and economic viability when imposing penalties (PDF p. 35).
- Where a high-risk system is modified by a new provider, the initial provider must cooperate and hand over documentation (PDF pp. 19 to 20, Art. 25).
- Candidate rules: classify every AI system DHCraft builds or deploys by AI Act role (provider, deployer) and risk class, record the dates above in the compliance calendar, and track the delegated acts and guidelines due by 2 August 2027 (PDF pp. 16, 34).

## Limits
- An amending regulation; its articles are only comprehensible together with the consolidated AI Act, which is not in this source.
- Reading the amended Art. 4 against the earlier wording relies on the recitals, which describe the old duty; the old text itself is not included in the source.
- Addresses providers and deployers of AI systems and enforcement authorities. It contains no rule on AI agents as a working method (such as coding agents), on research, funding or peer review.
- Large parts (Arts. 75 to 75d on the AI Office, annexes, amendments to Regulations 2018/1139 and 2023/1230) concern product legislation and enforcement and are of no practical relevance to DHCraft.
- The entry-into-force date is derived from the publication date, because the text only says "the third day following" publication.
