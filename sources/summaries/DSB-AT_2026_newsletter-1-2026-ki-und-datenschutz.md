# Newsletter der österreichischen Datenschutzbehörde Nr. 1/2026 (article "Updates zu KI und Datenschutz")

Publisher: Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde (DSB)
Date: 2026 (issue Nr. 1/2026; no day stated on the source; content refers to events up to 15 April 2026 and a role change on 1 April 2026)
Original: [DSB-AT_2026_newsletter-1-2026-ki-und-datenschutz.pdf](../DSB-AT_2026_newsletter-1-2026-ki-und-datenschutz.pdf)
Bindingness: Empfehlung (non-binding authority information; it restates binding law but is itself a newsletter)

## Core statement
A 15-page authority newsletter whose relevant part is a short article by a DSB department head (PDF pp. 3 to 5) on data protection and the AI Regulation when using AI tools. Its main position is that the GDPR applies in parallel to the AI Regulation and is not displaced by it, and that the AI Regulation is not a legal basis under Art. 6 GDPR. The remaining pages (decisions, court rulings, opinions on bills) are unrelated to AI.

## Relevance to the Leitfrage
### Client projects
- The GDPR applies whenever personal data is processed at least partly with automated support, including AI systems, and is "technologieneutral" (PDF p. 3). "Die DSGVO gilt parallel zur KI-VO und wird von ihr nicht verdrängt" (PDF p. 3).
- For every processing, also with AI tools, the Art. 5 principles apply: transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality. Controllers carry the burden of proof (Art. 5 para. 2) (PDF p. 3).
- Every processing needs one of the lawfulness grounds in Art. 6 para. 1 GDPR across the whole life cycle of the AI system. "Die KI-VO ist grundsätzlich keine Rechtsgrundlage iSd Art. 6 DSGVO" (PDF pp. 3 to 4).
- Data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure) must be preserved when AI systems are used, notably the right not to be subject to a solely automated individual decision including profiling (PDF p. 4).
- A data protection impact assessment under Art. 35 GDPR is described as "erforderlich und hilfreich" for AI systems whose effects on data subjects are hard to assess, and may be combined with the fundamental rights impact assessment under the AI Regulation (PDF p. 4).
- Candidate rule: before an agent touches client personal data, name the Art. 6 ground, document the Art. 5 principles and check whether a DPIA is needed.

### Research
The source says nothing specific on this field.

### Programming
The source says nothing specific on this field. It notes only that the technology must be examined where it affects storage duration or the exercise of data subject rights (PDF p. 3).

### Organisation
- The AI Regulation classifies AI systems by risk, subjects high-risk systems to requirements and conformity assessment, prohibits some practices, and regulates some systems hardly at all beyond transparency duties (PDF p. 4).
- Austria's national implementation of the AI Regulation, including designation of competent authorities, is still outstanding; the DSB is to be sectoral market surveillance authority for certain high-risk systems under Art. 74 para. 8 and was notified as fundamental rights authority under Art. 77 (PDF pp. 1 to 2, 4).
- The article remarks that autonomous AI agents "werden es möglicherweise bald sein" (ubiquitous) and that the DSB receives more complaints concerning AI systems (PDF p. 3).
- The Digital Omnibus proposals of November 2025 (a data, cybersecurity and privacy omnibus and an AI omnibus) were still under negotiation by the co-legislators; the AI omnibus foresees a possible postponement of high-risk obligations (PDF pp. 4 to 5). Outcome unknown at the time of writing.
- Candidate rule: keep a register of AI systems used and their risk class, and track the Omnibus outcome and the Austrian implementation law.

## Limits
- A newsletter article of a few paragraphs; no interpretation of the AI Regulation or GDPR beyond restating provisions, and no mention of agentic systems beyond one sentence.
- Addressed to the general public in Austria, not specifically to providers or small companies.
- The Omnibus status is a snapshot; the proposals were later adopted in some form (see the Official Journal source in this knowledge base), so this section is outdated on that point.
- The date is only given as year and issue number.
