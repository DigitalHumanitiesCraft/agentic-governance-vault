# Guidelines on Data Protection Officers ('DPOs'), WP 243 rev.01

Publisher: Article 29 Data Protection Working Party (secretariat provided by the European Commission)
Date: Adopted 13 December 2016, as last revised and adopted 5 April 2017
Original: [Art29WP_2017-04-05_wp243-guidelines-dpo.pdf](../Art29WP_2017-04-05_wp243-guidelines-dpo.pdf)
Bindingness: Empfehlung (guidance)
Note: Included as an approved exception to the 2026 date window, for the question of designating a data protection officer.

## Core statement

- Designation is mandatory in the three cases of Art. 37(1): public authority or body, core activities involving regular and systematic monitoring on a large scale, and core activities involving large-scale processing of special categories or criminal-conviction data (PDF p. 5).
- "Core activities" are the key operations needed to reach the organisation's goals, and include activities where processing is an inextricable part, while support functions such as payroll or standard IT support are usually ancillary (PDF p. 7).
- "Large scale" has no fixed number. The Working Party names four factors: number of data subjects, volume and range of data, duration, and geographical extent (PDF p. 7 to 8).
- Art. 37 applies to controllers and processors. A processor serving many small clients can reach large scale taken together, while a controller that meets the criteria does not automatically oblige its processor (PDF p. 9 to 10).
- Organisations should document their internal analysis of whether a DPO is required, and a voluntary designation is bound by Arts. 37 to 39 as if it were mandatory (PDF p. 5).
- A person with data protection tasks who is not a DPO must not carry that title (PDF p. 6).

## Relevance to the Leitfrage

### Client projects

- Examples of large-scale processing include patient data at a hospital, geo-location data processed by a processor specialised in such services, and data processing by telecoms providers. Examples of non-large-scale processing are patient data of an individual physician and conviction data of an individual lawyer (PDF p. 8).
- A processor "having many customers" can be large scale in aggregate (processor example, PDF p. 9 to 10). A small firm with few archive clients is not comparable on the text, which is an inference.
- The DPO of a processor also oversees the processor's own controller activities such as HR and IT (PDF p. 10).

### Research

- Footnote 14 cites Recital 91 and notes a large grey zone between the extremes of an individual professional and whole-country processing (PDF p. 7).
- Special categories include data on racial or ethnic origin, religious or philosophical beliefs, health and biometric data for identification (PDF p. 5, footnote 7). Testimony recordings may touch several of them, which is an inference.

### Programming

- Tracking and profiling are examples of monitoring, and the notion is not limited to the online environment (PDF p. 8). Agent tooling that profiles individuals would be assessed against this, which is an inference.
- Standard IT support is usually ancillary (PDF p. 7).

### Organisation

- Keep a written analysis of the designation question and update it when new activities or services start (PDF p. 5).
- A voluntary DPO may be an employee or external under a service contract, and must hold no role that determines purposes and means of processing, for example senior management or head of IT (PDF p. 12, p. 16).
- Staff or consultants who advise on data protection without being DPOs must have a clearly different title (PDF p. 6).

## Limits

- Guidance of the Article 29 Working Party, not binding law. The document text does not itself state the later endorsement by the EDPB.
- It predates the application of the GDPR (25 May 2018) and does not address generative AI or agentic systems.
- It gives no numeric thresholds and no rule for very small organisations.
- Section 3 on position and tasks was read only for conflicts of interest and external DPOs.
