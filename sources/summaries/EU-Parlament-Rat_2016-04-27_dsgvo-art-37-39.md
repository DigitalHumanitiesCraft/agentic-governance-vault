# Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Articles 37 to 39 and Recital 97 (excerpt)

Publisher: European Parliament and Council of the European Union (Official Journal L 119, 4.5.2016, via the Publications Office)
Date: 27 April 2016 (date of the act), published 4 May 2016
Original: [EU-Parlament-Rat_2016-04-27_dsgvo-art-37-39.md](../EU-Parlament-Rat_2016-04-27_dsgvo-art-37-39.md)
Bindingness: Pflicht (law)
Note: Included as an approved exception to the 2026 date window, for the question of designating a data protection officer.

## Core statement

- Controller and processor must designate a data protection officer where processing is done by a public authority or body, where core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale, or where core activities consist of large-scale processing of special categories (Art. 9) or criminal-conviction data (Art. 10) (Art. 37(1)(a) to (c)).
- Outside these cases designation is voluntary, unless Union or Member State law requires it (Art. 37(4)).
- The officer may be staff or an external service provider, must be chosen for professional qualities and expert knowledge, and the contact details must be published and communicated to the supervisory authority (Art. 37(5) to (7)).
- The officer must be involved in all data protection issues, receives no instructions on the tasks, reports to the highest management level, and may hold other tasks only without conflict of interests (Art. 38(1), (3), (6)).
- Tasks are to inform and advise, to monitor compliance, to advise on the impact assessment, and to cooperate with and act as contact for the supervisory authority (Art. 39(1)).
- In the private sector, core activities relate to primary activities, not to processing as an ancillary activity (Recital 97).

## Relevance to the Leitfrage

### Client projects

- Where DHCraft acts as processor, Art. 37(1) applies to it directly, so the test is applied to DHCraft's own core activities and not only to the client's (Art. 37(1), "the controller and the processor").
- Processing of testimony recordings or correspondence with special-category content triggers the duty only if that processing is a core activity and on a large scale (Art. 37(1)(c)).

### Research

- Research work for clients that touches special-category data falls under the same test. The text contains no research or archive exemption from Art. 37 (Art. 37(1)).

### Programming

- Agents that process personal data while building software are, on the text, ancillary support rather than a primary activity unless the product itself is that processing (Recital 97, sentence on ancillary activities). This is an inference from the recital.

### Organisation

- A voluntary designation is possible, and an external officer under a service contract is allowed (Art. 37(4), (6)).
- A designated officer needs resources, independence and published contact details, which shapes the internal rules for any such role (Art. 37(7), Art. 38(2), (3)).
- Roles that determine purposes and means of processing risk a conflict of interests (Art. 38(6)).

## Limits

- The excerpt contains only Recital 97 and Articles 37 to 39. It does not define "large scale", "core activities" or "regular and systematic monitoring", and Recital 91 and Art. 4 are not included.
- Art. 37(4) leaves room for national law, which is covered by the separate DSG source.
- The text gives no numeric thresholds and no exemption by company size (Art. 37(1)).
