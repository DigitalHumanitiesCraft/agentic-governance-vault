# Leitlinie zur Nutzung von Künstlicher Intelligenz in der Begutachtung (Guideline on the use of AI in peer review)

Publisher: Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG)
Date: 03/2026 (form number "DFG-Vordruck 4.04 - 03/26")
Original: [DFG_2026-03_leitlinie-ki-in-der-begutachtung.pdf](../DFG_2026-03_leitlinie-ki-in-der-begutachtung.pdf)
Bindingness: Vorgabe (funder requirement for reviewers; the text says AI use is "ausschließlich entlang der im Weiteren näher ausgeführten Prinzipien zulässig")

## Core statement
The four-page DFG guideline sets the conditions under which reviewers of DFG proposals may use AI systems. AI may only support the review, never replace the reviewer's own judgement. Four principles apply: confidentiality, transparency, quality assurance and responsibility.

## Relevance to the Leitfrage
### Client projects
The source says nothing specific on this field.

### Research
- AI is permitted only in a supporting sense; all substantive positions on the funding recommendation rest with the reviewer (PDF p. 2).
- Permitted examples are searching relevant literature, turning the reviewer's own notes into running text where the direction is already clear, and suggestions for structure or language of own content (PDF p. 2).
- Not permitted is generating significant parts of the review automatically and unreflectively, without own expert check (PDF p. 2).
- Confidentiality: proposal content may only be fed into an AI system if the provider does not store it permanently or beyond the concrete purpose (PDF p. 2). One of three conditions must hold: the system runs locally with no cloud transfer, or it is hosted by a trustworthy institution such as a university, or a cloud system is used under a licence agreement that guarantees data security, excludes use of inputs for training, and has the settings actively set, for example "kein logging" (PDF pp. 2 to 3).
- Cloud systems are named by example (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) and are allowed only under those contractual and configuration conditions (PDF p. 3).
- Systems based on documented, publicly available models with inspectable training data are recommended (PDF p. 3).
- Transparency: use of AI in a review must be disclosed with the specific purposes and extent, in a few sentences; marking individual passages is not required. "Eingesetzte KI, die sich nicht auf den (wissenschaftlichen) Inhalt des Gutachtens auswirkt" need not be disclosed, for example grammar, spell checking and translation (PDF p. 3).
- Quality assurance: uncritical adoption of AI-generated text is not permitted. Text must be checked for correctness, currency and bias, with explicit mention of status-quo bias and automation bias (PDF p. 3).
- Responsibility lies fully with the reviewer and cannot be transferred to an AI system (PDF p. 4).
- Passing on proposal content without authorisation, possibly by feeding it into LLMs that use inputs as training data, can breach confidentiality and constitute scientific misconduct under § 9 Nr. 2 VerfOwF (PDF p. 4).
- Candidate rule for DHCraft when it acts as reviewer for a DFG procedure: no proposal text goes into any AI system unless one of the three confidentiality conditions is verified, and AI use is disclosed with purpose and extent.

### Organisation
- The guideline justifies itself by reference to the AI Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the GDPR and copyright law (PDF p. 1). It anchors its principles in DFG code Leitlinie 16 on confidential treatment of material under review (PDF p. 4).
- Candidate organisational rule: a register of approved AI services with their contractual training exclusion and logging settings, usable as evidence of the confidentiality condition.

## Limits
- Addressed to reviewers of DFG proposals only, not to applicants, and not to FWF or other funders.
- Does not say how a trustworthy hosting institution is to be assessed or how "kein logging" settings are verified.
- Does not cover agentic tools, tool use or code; it speaks of AI systems in general.
- The text names the legal grounds only in passing and does not interpret them.
