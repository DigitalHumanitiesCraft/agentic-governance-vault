# Project

## Leitfrage

> Welche Regeln braucht DHCraft, damit AI Agents in Projekten, Forschung und Programmierung regelkonform, sicher und nachprüfbar arbeiten?

Which rules does DHCraft need so that AI agents work lawfully, securely and verifiably in projects, research and programming? The operator chose this question on 2026-11-05 from three proposals. The two alternatives each covered one half of the sources: one asked only what DHCraft must observe legally and towards funders, the other only how to use agents securely and verifiably in programming and research.

## Profile

The operator is a researcher and the CEO of Digital Humanities Craft (DHCraft), a small research and software company in Graz, Austria. EU law, the GDPR and the rules of Austrian and European research funders therefore apply.

DHCraft wants to use AI agents in three fields, and the Leitfrage names all three.

- Client projects, where agents may handle client material and personal data.
- Research, including funding proposals and peer review.
- Programming, where agents write and change code in repositories.

## Scope

The knowledge base rests only on sources published in 2026, with the date verified on the source. One exception applies. On 2026-11-05 the operator approved including the legal base text and the authoritative older guidelines for the question whether DHCraft must designate a data protection officer, because no 2026 source states the criteria of Article 37 GDPR. Such sources are marked "exception" in the date column of quellen.md. How sources were selected and checked is described in [INDEX.md](INDEX.md), the sources themselves in [quellen.md](quellen.md). The answer to the Leitfrage is [leitlinien-dhcraft.md](leitlinien-dhcraft.md).

## Offene Punkte

- The AI Act base text of 2024 lies outside the date window. Statements on its unchanged articles rest on the 2026 Commission guidelines that interpret them.
- Digital Humanities workflows are covered only in part. A peer-reviewed study evaluates LLM encoding of scholarly editions in TEI, and a preprint describes agent-assisted historical source work. Other DH workflows such as transcription, entity linking or archival description have no 2026 source, and the other empirical studies are preprints.
- The Austrian Agency for Research Integrity (ÖAWI) published a 2026 guideline on good scientific practice with explicit rules on AI use. No Austrian authority published a dated 2026 document on agents under data protection law or the AI Act. The FAQ of the KI-Servicestelle at RTR carries 2026 content but no date, and Austria has not yet designated its AI Act authorities.
- No EDPB or EDPS guidance on agentic AI and no 2026 source from ENISA or OWASP within the window was found. A 2026 exploratory note of the French CNIL and CIANum covers agentic AI under the GDPR as an exploratory note, no guideline or recommendation of the CNIL; guidelines of EDPB and Commission on the interplay of GDPR and AI Act were still being drafted at its publication. A 2026 security information of the German BSI addresses AI-driven attacks on organisations and, in one section, prompt injection and poisoning in agentic systems; it is no guidance on agent security as such. The FWF source is a news item and no guideline document.
- The labelling of published AI-generated text is now covered in practical detail by the 2026 Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content, drawn up by working-group chairs and published by the Commission according to its press release. It binds only its signatories, and whether the Commission and the AI Board assessed it as adequate is not recorded in the base.
- Review and test practice for agent-written code is covered at the level of principles by the OpenSSF and CNCF guide, the ReSA report and an NCSC-UK blog post, and one preprint measures security smells in agent pull requests. No 2026 source describes a detailed review and test procedure.
- On 2026-11-05 the operator decided that the governance names no individual services, leaves the concrete tools to the tool register and does not describe current agent practice. DHCraft works mainly with public-domain or freely usable humanities research data; personal data occur rarely, and the rules for them apply to those cases. The governance is a Beschlussvorlage 1.0 that takes effect once both owners adopt it. On the same day the operator decided to publish the workshop results under DigitalHumanitiesCraft on GitHub Pages: the card workspace, the governance PDF, the pitch deck and the Northwind exercise page. Company name and operator may be named; real company or client data may not be used. Originals and full texts of sources stay unpublished because they belong to their publishers.
